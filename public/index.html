01 · The Gap

Moving a tokenized asset between two regulated institutions in Canada today is slow, manual, and legally uncertain.

Trades take days to settle. Capital sits locked up while everyone waits. And projects that want to tokenize real assets have nowhere trusted to plug into. Canada already has the assets, the custodians, and a tokenized-cash starting point. What it does not have is a neutral settlement layer that makes a transfer final and legal across institutions, in seconds instead of days. Until one exists, tokenized finance in Canada either does not move between institutions, or it clears through infrastructure Canada does not own.

Market structure

Title without finality

An asset can be tokenized, but ownership cannot change hands across two institutions in a way that is final and legal without a settlement layer that issues the authoritative record of title.

Cash and asset move apart

Without delivery-versus-payment, one side pays and waits for the other to deliver. The multi-day gap, the reconciliation after the fact, and the counterparty risk stay exactly where they are.

Rules checked after the fact

On disconnected ledgers, identity, eligibility, and sanctions are verified after a transfer rather than embedded in it, so a transfer that should not complete still can, and compliance is rebuilt at every hop.

Canada imports its infrastructure

With no domestic neutral operator, Canadian tokenized finance routes through foreign venues and rails. The activity, the talent, and the fee revenue clear somewhere else.

02 · The Proof

The settlement model is proven in principle. The open question is who operates it for Canadian institutions.

The standard objection was that regulators would not allow tokenized settlement and that institutions would not show up. In 2026, both objections weakened, in Canada, with named counterparties, inside the regulatory perimeter.

Bank of Canada · Project Samara · Mar 6, 2026
$100M

A tokenized bond issued by Export Development Canada, with RBC and TD, settled end to end in Bank of Canada wholesale central-bank money (W-CAD). Full lifecycle: issuance, bidding, coupon payments, secondary trading, redemption.

Samara settled against central-bank money, not a commercial-bank deposit token; 4orm's model uses a tokenized commercial-bank deposit as the cash leg. Directional support only. 4orm is not affiliated with the Bank of Canada or the Samara consortium. Bank of Canada Staff Analytical Paper 2026-8.
Why it matters to 4orm

Samara established the architectural patterns 4orm builds against: title and cash settling together, inside the regulatory perimeter, with real institutions. It is directional support that settlement finality for tokenized assets works in Canada. What it does not answer is who operates the neutral layer for commercial-bank settlement day to day. That is the company.

FEB 3, 2026
CIRO publishes the Digital Asset Custody Framework, the permission structure for regulated custody of tokenized assets in Canada. The custody question now has a rulebook.
MAR 2026
The CSA launches Project Tokenization, a national workstream inviting industry to build tokenized financial products with the regulators, not around them.
The pattern in Canadian market infrastructure is validate, permit, build. Samara gave directional support. CIRO published the custody framework. The CSA opened the door. What Canada still does not have is a neutral settlement layer that makes transfers final across institutions. The only question is whether the operator that builds it is Canadian-owned and regulator-aligned, or whether Canada imports its settlement layer the way it imported its cloud.
03 · The Platform

4orm Finance: the neutral settlement layer for Canadian tokenized real-world assets.

4orm is settlement-layer software and the authoritative record of title that makes a transfer final and legal across two institutions. It issues the record of title and connects to a tokenized bank deposit, held by a regulated Canadian bank as the bank's own liability, for the cash leg. Developed by KCS Capital; operated by 4orm Finance as a separately governed, regulated Alberta entity. Phase 1 is proof of architecture and two targeted Memoranda of Understanding.

4orm is
  • Settlement-layer software and the record of title for tokenized assets.
  • The layer that makes a transfer final and legal across two institutions.
  • The connector to a tokenized bank deposit that provides the cash leg.
  • A neutral, independently governed Canadian entity no single bank owns.
4orm is not
  • Not a bank, and does not take deposits (Bank Act).
  • Not a custodian of customer assets (custody is a separate proposed entity, 4orm Trust Co).
  • Not a stablecoin issuer (the deposit token is the bank's liability).
  • Not the AML officer of record (the regulated institutions keep their obligations).
