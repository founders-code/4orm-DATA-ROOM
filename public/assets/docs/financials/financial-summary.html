3 · How we validate it

Comparable evidence sets the scale. A paid pilot proves it on real files.

The need is driven by rules already in force. The savings are shown by comparable tools. What remains is to prove 4orm's own price, volume and reuse with paying firms, on their own files.

Already evidenced

  • Regulators require the record now: RPAA safeguarding is live and penalised, mortgage firms carry ongoing FINTRAC duties. Bank of Canada ↗ FINTRAC ↗
  • 100% of a targeted FSRA sample had no documented suitability assessment. Evidence ↗
  • Comparable tools cut work from days to hours and hours to minutes, with named cases. Evidence book ↗
  • Adjacent "prove trust and compliance" businesses already reached large recurring revenue (below).

Still to prove

  • The C$25 blended Decision Record price, on the C$15 / C$25 / C$39 validation ladder.
  • 1.5M paid records by 2031, the hardest volume assumption.
  • 30 enterprise / network customers at C$400K, and 20 API customers at C$350K.
  • At least 70% architecture reuse into the second vertical.

How the pilot settles it. A paid pilot measures review minutes per case, retrieval time for a file, rework and follow-up, and consumer understanding, on the firm's own files. A design-partner firm co-proves the record before the networks carry it. These are the measures that turn the target into proof.

Comparables show the scale, not a promise. These are published figures for other companies in adjacent trust and compliance markets. They show category buyers already pay at this scale, including a Canadian precedent. They do not predict 4orm's own adoption, pricing or revenue.

4 · How investors get paid

A share of profit, paid from profit, starting the year it turns positive.

For the first five years, 40% of the company's positive after-tax profit is paid to the investor pool, allocated by dollars invested rather than by equity. The company funds it from profit and stays cash-positive after the payments.

Step 1
Profit appears
Profit before tax turns positive in 2028 and grows to C$56.1M by 2031.
Step 2
40% to the pool
Forty percent of positive after-tax profit goes to the investor pool, first five years.
Step 3
Split by dollars in
Each investor’s share is their invested dollars over the participating capital.
Step 4
Paid, cash stays positive
Cash after tax and distributions still grows to C$29.4M by 2031.

How to read it. Your cash that year is really paid: it is this cheque’s C$100K share of the C$7.5M participating capital, applied to 40% of the company’s after-tax profit. Cash received so far adds those up, reaching C$345,496 by 2031. Your equity value is the stake this C$100K bought, not sold, valued at an illustrative 5× that year’s licence revenue; it is accessed at a sale or a later round, not paid out yearly. Total you could access is the two together.

Access each year: the cash column is distributed and spendable; the equity column is value on paper until an exit. The profit-share does not stop in 2031 – the plan proposes a further 20% of after-tax profit across 2032 to 2036, with those profits not yet forecast, so they are not shown here.

These are management illustrations, not promised returns. Payments require available cash and board approval. Returns depend on the modelled seed closing, on tax, and on a valuation multiple that is an assumption. Tax is an illustrative 27%.
