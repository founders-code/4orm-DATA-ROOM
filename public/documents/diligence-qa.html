Do you have any customers today?

No. There is no software live and no paying customer as at August 2026. The build partner is at a signed letter of intent. The first paying customer is a target for early 2027, and ten installed reference customers is a target for early 2028.

What is the early price a multiple of?

Nothing yet. There is no revenue to divide by today, so every early price is set against a forecast. Only the 2028 round has real revenue behind it, at about one and a half times the money coming in every year.

Who competes with you?

Twenty three products were priced against this market. Four of them reach both places the work needs, the bank account and the daily list of who is owed what, and all four were built for the British rules. None of them carries the Canadian one.

The plan runs short of cash. What closes it?

The account is overdrawn in eight of the months before the 2028 round, lowest in May 2028 at about $779,000 short. The cheapest fix is the optional $500,000 business loan, which narrows the lowest month to about $341,000 short, costs about $90,000 of interest and no ownership, and is repaid out of the round.

Can you actually build and deliver it?

The build runs through an outside partner who starts on signature and stops on completion. That is why the plan reaches a working product on this smaller round rather than needing the larger round to begin. Headcount grows from three people at the start to forty seven by 2031.

Are the market numbers real, or estimated?

The market was counted one firm at a time: 18,254 Canadian firms, of which 745 already hold customer money today. The plan is honest that thirteen of the thirty two rows behind that count are primary sourced and the rest are management estimates, so any share measured against an estimated row carries an estimated bottom number.

How does an investor get paid if nobody ever sells?

From 2029 the company plans to pay out forty per cent of its profit after tax. The investors are paid first and in full until they have had four times their money back in cash, and the founding shares get nothing before that. The shares are still held after the cash, and no sale is assumed anywhere in the figures.

What would make the price wrong?

Five things the plan says about itself, not objections a reader has to dig for: the eight overdrawn months, only about one month of cash in hand at the 2028 decision, no customer under contract yet, the most expensive customer tier having the thinnest evidence, and an earnings margin that cannot be quoted yet because the cost base has not been rebuilt for every revenue line.
