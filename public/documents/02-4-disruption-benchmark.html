Track record test

What Similar Software Companies Actually Captured

This paper places the plan against twenty software companies that arrived behind a new rule, to test whether its target is realistic. The pattern is clear: where the regulator published a list of who was bound, vendors reached 14 to 31 per cent of it; where the population was only an estimate, they stopped near 1 per cent. The plan aims for 28.7 per cent of 745 named firms, which sits near the top of the proven band. The paper also names the one number it cannot support from anyone else's record: the price each firm will pay.

A list beats an estimate

The one thing that split the winners from the losers was not product quality, rule severity or market size. It was whether a salesperson could open a published list and count the names on it. The gap between the two groups is about twenty times.

The plan sits inside the proven band

The plan crosses one benchmark vendor's first-year result in 2029 and its third-year result in 2030, landing at 28.7 per cent in 2031, below the top precedent of 31 per cent and above the others.

Three tests pass, one has no anchor

The population is published, the share is inside the observed band, and the duty cannot be waited out. The fourth test, price per obligated firm, has no comparable anywhere, so it stands on its own and is the exposed assumption.

A warning case up front

Under one identical Italian invoicing rule, three vendors saw yearly revenue grow 22.7 per cent, 0.7 per cent, and shrink 2 per cent. A severe rule does not on its own make a vendor win; distribution does.
