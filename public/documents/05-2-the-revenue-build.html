Where the Revenue Comes From

All of the revenue in the plan comes from six product lines plus services sold alongside them, and each line has its own price, its own take-up ramp and its own on/off switch. The largest by far is the daily record a customer subscribes to, at $52,279,213 in 2031. Total revenue is $105,276,946 in 2031, of which $96,564,640 repeats.

The daily subscription carries the plan

Every day the system reads what the bank holds against what the books say each customer is owed, records the answer and seals it. That sealed daily record is the subscription, and it is the biggest line at $52,279,213 in 2031.

The cheapest tier carries the most money

The smallest-firm tier holds 631 of the 835 customers and $20,666,383 of the revenue. The risk is signing that many firms, not what each pays: cutting the price a fifth costs only 4.7 per cent of 2031 revenue.

One line is built to shrink

Rebuilding records a firm never kept falls from $2,466,000 in 2028 to $1,941,306 in 2031, because each year the gap of missing history gets shorter. The workbook fails a check if that line ever grows.

The most uncertain line is quoted switched off

Charging firms that owe proof to a second regulator reaches $13,766,769 in 2031, but no published Canadian figure exists for how many firms owe two duties. Switched off, 2031 revenue is $91,510,177 and no check breaks.
