Who is building this

The Team and Partners

This document introduces the people building 4orm Finance and explains why they come first. Compliance software is bought on credibility before features, so an investor is buying the bench. Two founders run the company, a systems architect and a technical chief support the build, six advisors each own one named area, and ten engineers at the build partner are named on paper so they can be checked.

What you are looking to know

  • Who runs the company day to day, and what each founder owns.
  • Whether the people selling to regulated buyers are credible enough to get the meeting.
  • Who is advising on the money side, and whether one person holds too much of it.

  • Who writes the software, and whether that team has shipped in hard places before.
  • What still has to be hired, and what this round pays for.

The two founders

Chad R. Johnston is founder and chief executive; he came from life safety work in oil and gas, then years building operational systems in Canadian financial education, and he owns the customer relationship, the round and the scope. Kevin Wong is co-founder for go to market, with twenty two years scaling companies as a founder and part-time marketing chief, and he owns positioning, the sales channel and market expansion.

The build seats

Zahiruddin Sandeela is the systems architect, with twenty years building regulated systems including payments and anti money laundering work at a Canadian credit union. Mathew Mozaffari, founder and chief executive of the build partner Speer Technologies, holds the technical chief seat while the software is built, so the company gets that function without paying the salary yet.

Advisors who own the money and the law

Bruce Fair brings institutional market access, James Atherton advises on founder structuring and the private raise, Miika Makela owns how the plan is priced, and Don H put in the first cheque at incorporation. On the legal side, Fasken owns the technology and corporate framework while Capiche Legal owns founder structuring and the raise, so the two files sit with two firms on purpose.

What this round hires, and what it does not

Nothing is built before two senior hires are in place: an engineering lead and a part-time regulatory lead, kept separate so a coder never makes a rules decision and a rules author never ships code. The engineering team at Speer works under a phased contract with published rates and a one month exit at every stage, and the company plans for external engineering of about $825,000 across 2026 and 2027 and $6,736,391 across the whole plan.
