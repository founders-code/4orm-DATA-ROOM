The Closest Competitor, AutoRek

AutoRek is the nearest thing in the world to what 4orm is building, so this three-page paper studies how it won and where it has left doors open. It is a 32-year-old British firm that sells the same shape of software, reconciling money held for customers, with about £28.0M of yearly revenue growing 34 per cent a year, and it holds only about 1.5 per cent of its own market. It won by building on a rule with a deadline and a named officer, then marketing patiently. Its weak spots are that it is almost invisible on the review sites and analyst lists buyers now use, has never published a price in 32 years, and its record can only be trusted because the vendor says so.

A regional firm that markets like a leader

AutoRek looks larger than it is. Its defensible ground is British firms with a client-money duty, where it is a credible top three, so every claim it makes should be read against that narrow scale.

Four doors left open

It has one online review after 32 years and no analyst rating, no published price, no trial and no self-serve, a British-weighted customer base with a 17-month-old United States office, and a record that only proves anything if you trust AutoRek itself.

Most of these are cheap to fix

Fifty verified reviews and an analyst briefing would close the visibility gap in two quarters. The one gap it cannot fix without rebuilding what it spent 32 years on is that its record cannot be proven untouched to an outside party.

The separating question

AutoRek keeps a very good record and asks the regulator to trust that it was never altered. The question that changes any evaluation is: who has to be trusted for this record to stand up?
