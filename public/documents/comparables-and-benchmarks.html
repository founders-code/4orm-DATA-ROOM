Who Is Already Here, and Who Could Arrive

This paper reads 23 competing products on their own published material and finds the market splits cleanly in two, which is the heart of the case. Every vendor with a real Canadian presence sells money-laundering checks or data plumbing and never touches money held for customers. Every vendor that handles held money properly is British and built for British rules. Nobody sits in the middle, so the paper names the gaps, ranks the firms most likely to close them, and says what each would have to build or buy first.

A market split down the middle

Products that watch a company's systems and collect proof never see money. Products that match two sets of numbers carry no compliance duty. Only four products reach both the bank and the customer ledger and compute a daily position, and all four are built for British rules.

The threats, ranked

Cable, now inside Synctera, has the shortest path because Synctera already runs in Canada with a sponsor bank and Cable's pitch is auditor-ready evidence; it would only need to build the daily ledger. AutoRek needs only a Canadian rule layer and a sales presence. Workiva, Diligent and Duco each face a bigger build or a smaller market than they want.

Why 4orm can be first

No vendor holds a Canadian safeguarding customer today, so there is no incumbent to displace. The British specialists have never opened a third country, and the evidence platforms would have to build a whole ledger before a Canadian rule badge means anything.

What would prove the position wrong

AutoRek could ship, without saying so, a Canadian module, or Synctera could push Cable into safeguarding. The answer to every threat is the same: named Canadian customers with a signed price on a record a reviewer has accepted.
