THE OPPORTUNITY

Canada settles tomorrow what the world settles in seconds

When a tokenized asset moves between two Canadian institutions, it cannot become final and legal today. The token is not the hard part; making the transfer final, legal, and recognized on the other side is. The direction is proven abroad by the UK Regulated Liability Network and Fnality, and at home by the Bank of Canada's Project Samara, which in March 2026 settled a C$100M tokenized bond with RBC, TD, and Export Development Canada under OSC, AMF, and CIRO sign-off. No one is building the neutral, bank-integrated, Canadian-dollar settlement layer that connects these pieces.

What 4orm is, and is not

4orm is the regulated record of title and rules layer that makes a transfer final across two institutions: it issues the ledger of record and token registry and enforces identity, eligibility, sanctions, and jurisdiction rules on transfer. 4orm is not a bank, a deposit-taker, a custodian, a stablecoin issuer, or the AML officer of record. The cash-leg deposit liability is issued and held by the chartered bank; custody is held by a licensed custodian; KYC and AML sit with the bank and custodian.

2.0
THE MODEL

Three things travel with any asset

For a transfer to be final across institutions, three things must settle together: title (the record of ownership, which 4orm issues), the cash leg (a tokenized bank deposit, issued by the bank as its own liability), and the rules (identity, eligibility, sanctions, and jurisdiction, enforced on transfer). When all three settle together, delivery-versus-payment, the transfer is final the moment it commits.

4orm issues the record of title, not the money. This separation is deliberate: it keeps 4orm out of deposit-taking and custody, and positions it as neutral infrastructure that no single bank owns. The product line is interoperability and settlement finality.

3.0
THE WEDGE

Why bank deposits first

Every settlement has a cash leg; an asset cannot be swapped without money on the other side. A tokenized bank deposit is that cash leg, in regulated Canadian dollars institutions already trust. The deposit is not a small first step before the real thing; it is the on-ramp that pulls everything else through. Own the cash leg and 4orm becomes the settlement asset every other transfer needs: bonds, receivables, real estate, and eventually stablecoins from other issuers.

4.0
THE MARKET

A large, sourced, addressable base

Canadian institutional settlement runs on batch systems that confirm the next day, carrying float, reconciliation, and compliance duplication as a permanent cost. The addressable base is the population of regulated Canadian institutions that would settle tokenized assets against a bank-issued cash leg. Market sizing, segmentation, and the institution-by-institution savings model are set out in the market documents.

All market figures in the room are labeled as verified third-party data or as management estimates with methodology, and are cross-referenced to their source. See Market Sizing (document 02.1) and the Canadian RWA Landscape (document 02.4).

THE BUILD

Four phases, forward-looking

Phase 1
The cash leg. Stand up the tokenized bank deposit as the settling money.
Phase 2
Interoperability proof. Settle a second asset against the cash leg.
Phase 3
Conditional settlement. A verified milestone releases payment instantly.
Phase 4
Open network. Other issuers plug in and settle through 4orm.

At launch, 4orm supports primary issuance and bilateral delivery-versus-payment settlement only. It does not operate a marketplace, exchange, or trading venue at launch; any secondary-trading or marketplace function is a later-phase capability contingent on recognition and registration under NI 21-101, NI 23-101, NI 24-102, and NI 31-103, and on exemptive relief where required.

THE MODEL ECONOMICS

SaaS-led, contracted, and conservatively rated

The five-year model is SaaS-led: software and integration fees plus issuance fees are the durable base, with settlement and registry fees as upside. Every rate is set below what comparable services already charge. All figures are management estimates from the model, not contracted amounts; methodology and downside cases are in the model itself.

Modeled five-year platform revenue is approximately C$101.8M, rising to about C$104.0M including non-dilutive grants; the base case does not rely on an illustrative settlement-savings line. Roughly 72% is SaaS and issuance, contracted streams that do not depend on trading volume. The custody line is a contracted share of a licensed custodian's fee, not custody revenue earned by 4orm. See Revenue Drivers (document 05.3) and the Five-Year Model (document 05.1).

8.0
REGULATORY POSTURE

Built inside the perimeter, honestly staged

4orm structures custodian and dealer functions separately by design, a deliberate structuring choice consistent with CIRO's custody-tiering principles, not a structure mandated by CIRO. The work is informed by, but not a participant in, the Bank of Canada's Project Samara and the CSA's Project Tokenization initiative. 4orm is not a money services business and not the AML officer of record; if 4orm triggers FINTRAC registration it will register before commencing that function.

4orm Finance is not yet incorporated, registered, recognized, or authorized except as stated; all regulatory statuses are pathways, not approvals. The entity status is set out in Entity Status (document 08.7).
