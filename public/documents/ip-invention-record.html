The Record of What We Invented

This is the private file prepared for the company's patent lawyers, never placed in the data room. It sets out the invention worth protecting, gathers the dated evidence the company already has, maps the closest existing work a lawyer should search, and names the eleven questions only the inventors can answer. The invention is an unchangeable record that can produce any past period again, a year later, landing on an identical copy, which is the harder thing a regulator's outside reviewer actually tests.

The claim is reproducing the past, not just storing it

Many systems can make a record tamper-evident. Producing the same past period twice, a year apart, and landing on an identical copy is a different and harder requirement, and it is the part the written record suggests is new.

An honest map of prior work

The file lists the closest existing ideas, including linked timestamping from a 1991 paper and today's ledger databases, and says where each one stops short. It is candid that some pieces of the invention are old on their own and only the combination may be defensible.

Gaps named, not filled in

Where a detail has not been written down yet, the file marks it unfinished rather than inventing something plausible. The most urgent gap is the exact method that makes two exports identical, which is the strongest element and the least documented.

Ownership and timing to settle

The inventors are not yet named on file, and the build was arranged so the build partner never touched the protected layers, which keeps ownership simple. A build pack dated 5 August 2026 went out before the confidentiality agreement was signed on 13 August 2026, and the company needs to confirm who saw it.
