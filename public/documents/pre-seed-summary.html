WHAT SEPARATES THEM
· Base. The channel converts. One hundred and ten network partners by 2031 carry most of the professional seats, and the regulated wedge lands at a normal pace. Sixty people in 2031.
· Low. Adoption works and customers stay, but distribution takes far longer to build. A quarter of the channel conversion, a sixth of the revenue, and no profitable year inside the plan.
· Blue-sky. Two or three national networks or platforms adopt it and adoption stops being linear. It needs the most capital and the most people, and it is the upside case rather than the plan.
· The same product, the same price card and the same law in all three. What changes is how fast distribution is built, which is why the first named network partner is the checkpoint that matters most.

WHAT THIS TABLE DOES NOT CLAIM
· The first named network partner is not signed. Most of the professional growth from 2028 arrives through networks in every case, so if that slips the plan moves toward the Low column and the answer is to slow the hiring plan rather than to raise earlier.
· It stops at 2029. The master pro forma runs to 2031 and is available.
· It counts no grant and no research credit. Eight programmes will be in progress and none is awarded.
· It counts nothing from referral, second regulator expansion or international. All three are switched off in the master pro forma and carry no sizing at all.
· One number is deliberately missing: how long a new customer takes to get working. It can only be observed, and the whole gross margin case rests on it.
· Base assumes 12,000 paying professionals by 2029. That is 5.5 per cent of a sourced professional population of 217,585, and it is the assumption most worth attacking.
· Blue-sky does not reach the September 2027 round on the pre-seed alone. It spends faster than it earns in 2027 and comes up about C$458,000 short. That is on the Cash Plan page and it is not hidden.

WHAT WE ARE NOT COUNTING, AND WHY
· Credit unions and banks. They hold deposits as a debt they owe the customer, not as money held in trust for them, so the duty is a different one.
· Auto and equipment finance. No Canadian law requiring a trust account was found, so nothing is counted.
· Cemetery operators. Ontario lists more than five thousand and most are small church or municipal grounds, so the count would flatter the total without adding reachable customers.
· Alberta and Quebec. Alberta publishes a single combined licence holder total rather than a brokerage count, and Quebec is not counted at all.
· Every province other than Ontario and British Columbia in layer two, apart from the three national figures.
· Law firms are already cut by a fifth to allow for firms holding no trust money. If that cut is too generous the layer is smaller; if it is too harsh the layer is larger.
The plan does not need this market to be large. It needs the second layer to be reachable with the same software as the first, because that is what turns one regulated product into a category rather than a niche.
