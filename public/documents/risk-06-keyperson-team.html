Key-person risk

Reliance on Key People

This asks whether a three person company that contracts out most of its delivery is a single point of failure, and whether the rules it sets for itself survive its own growth. Every role is defined twice: by what it owns and by what it may not do. Those separations are the product as much as the software is, because a record signed by whoever was available is not evidence. The risk is that three people hold these lines in 2026 and forty seven hold them in 2031, against seventy nine contracted.

More contracted people than employees

Employees reach 47 by 2031 while contracted delivery reaches 79. Four things are never handed to an outside partner: the add-only record store, the two dates, the filing packs and the rules. Everything built belongs to the company the day it is made.

One senior engineer until 2028

A single senior engineer owns two of the layers nobody else may build through 2026 and 2027. The control is a written architecture note that moves the design out of one head, plus a named backup reviewer so acceptance is never one person's signature.

Rules that cost nothing now, everything later

No one holds a standing login to live data, and no one is ever both preparer and approver on a release or filing. A rule that cannot be enforced at three people will not be enforced at forty seven, so the discipline is built while it costs nothing.

Paid above the market, on purpose

The blended cost is $142,000 a person against Canadian benchmarks of $66,955 to $97,000. So there is no cost lever left in the team line, and the real control is time to fill: any senior role open longer than a quarter during the scale-up is escalated.
