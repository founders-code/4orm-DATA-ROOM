Building the Software

This asks whether the plan's single engine survives real bank feeds, a second regulator and a delivery deadline. The whole cost case rests on one sentence: everything above the core software is content, a setting or a list of fields, not new code. Four parts of the record cannot be added later, and the platform claim is only proven the moment it is tested, which is scheduled for the third quarter of 2028, the same milestone the 2028 round is priced on.

Build the invisible parts first

Four features cost days or weeks to build now and are close to impossible to add later: a record store that can only be added to, two dates on every record, rules held as writing, and customers kept apart at the key. None of them shows in a demonstration, which is why they get traded away for a demo date.

The second-regulator test

Adding a second regulator on the same engine is scheduled for the third quarter of 2028 and carries $13,766,769 of 2031 revenue. The plan's advice is to run that test early, in 2027, on an awkward duty, rather than discover the answer in the quarter the round is priced.

Prove it by watching it run

The core test is exporting a period again a year later and getting identical bytes. That, plus running any day again and getting the same answer, can be shown running rather than described, which is the only useful assurance at this stage.

What the software refuses to do

The helper tool may search inside one customer but may never put an invented value into the record, and a person signs everything. The design holds no money and moves no money, which keeps the company outside the rules and penalties that fall on its customers.
