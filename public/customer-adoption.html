Customer adoption and distribution.
The plan reaches forty thousand paid professionals and one hundred and ten payment firms by 2031. None of it is sold one office at a time. These markets already sit inside networks, franchisors and dealer groups, so one relationship reaches many firms at once. This document shows how that works, and the evidence and sources behind it.

How a market is won, not one office at a time
1
Networks and franchisors
  • One agreement with a network reaches the firms inside it, so the record becomes the group standard.
  • Adoption spreads inside the group once the first firms are live and can be referenced.
  • The cost to win a network is paid once; the firms behind it arrive at a fraction of it.
2
Dealer groups and lenders
  • Automotive and mortgage sit inside dealer groups and lender panels that already set requirements.
  • When the buyer sets the record as a condition, the professionals follow without a separate sale.
  • The evidence a lender wants is the same evidence the regulator wants.
3
The free consumer layer
  • The 4ormIQ check is free, so consumers arrive first and at no cost to acquire.
  • A business a consumer has checked is already pulled toward the record.
  • Demand from the person moves the firm, rather than the firm being pushed.
Distribution is already organised.
One relationship reaches many firms, which is why the plan does not depend on a field sales team calling offices one at a time. The channel is the network, not the door.

A counted population, not a headline market size.
Forty thousand seats is 18.4 per cent of 217,585 sourced professionals. Three million consumers is 7.2 per cent of the adult population. A number that is a share of something counted can be argued with, rather than asserted.
In plain terms
Many plans quote a giant market and claim a small slice of it. This one does the opposite: it starts from the actual list of firms and professionals who now owe this duty, and shows what fraction the plan needs to reach. Smaller, but real and checkable.

What it costs to win a customer, by route
Drawn from zero to one scale, so the length is the cost. A direct seat is cheap and pays for itself in 6.6 months; a network costs more once and returns the firms behind it.

Why the distribution model holds
These markets are concentrated by design. Mortgage brokers work under a small number of networks; car dealers sit inside dealer groups; payment firms are a defined list of 745. Reaching a fifth of them is a task of signing a handful of the right relationships, not of tens of thousands of individual sales.

What would make this wrong
If these markets were not organised into networks, the reach would need a far larger sales team and the costs would change. If a named product already carried this record, the timing would be overstated. Both are worth testing, and both are cheaper to settle now than later.
