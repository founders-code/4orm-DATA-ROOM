The Challenge · In the Data

The pressure is rising, and documentation alone is not keeping up.

Published Canadian evidence points to the same gap: firms face more compliance work, yet a file on its own does not show that a decision was understood, reviewed in time, or explained. Each figure below is shown with the source and with its limits.

2

What each signal means, and what it does not.

Rising workload · PwC
94% of Canadian respondents to PwC's 2025 survey reported greater compliance complexity over the prior three years, against 85% globally.
The pressure extends beyond any single rule change.Limit: this measures how executives perceive complexity, not a measured increase in spending.
Cost signal · Insurance Bureau of Canada
Reported compliance costs rose 81% from 2022 to 2024, compliance staff rose 26%, and internal labour made up nearly three quarters of the cost.
The burden is largely staff time, which is where software can free capacity.Limit: 24 companies, about 61% of the Canadian property and casualty market. One segment, not all financial firms.
A control gap · FSRA
In FSRA's large-brokerage examinations, checks were document-focused and most file reviews happened after closing.
Having a file does not show timely, meaningful oversight.Limit: findings apply to the firms examined, not a market-wide failure rate.
Verification needs · CRA
Of 1,637 mortgage-industry submissions, 47% said a yes/no income check would not meet their needs (41% said yes, 12% unsure).
Different cases need different depth of verification, not one questionnaire for all.Limit: consultation interest is not the same as willingness to pay, and no such connected service was shown to exist today.
Authority must be checked · CSA
11,728 fraudulent investment and crypto websites were deactivated in one year under the CSA / CIRO initiative (5 June 2025 to 5 June 2026).
A convincing document is not, by itself, evidence of authorization.Limit: an enforcement-output count, not a count of victims, businesses or distinct fraud networks.

4

How to read these figures.

Regulator publications establish rules, guidance and examination findings within their scope. Consultations and surveys describe the people who took part. Fraud and enforcement counts describe incidents and activity, not a software market. International cases (for example the UK Consumer Duty) show that related tools attract investment abroad; they are not Canadian requirements, Canadian results or forecasts for 4orm. None of these figures is presented as a measure of 4orm's results. Every one is sourced, dated and carried with its limit in the research paper.
