The case

Why 4orm, and Why Now

How the record went missing, what it costs the four parties who live with it, and why the window to fix it is open now rather than later.

The claim

The record does not exist. The law now assumes it does.

Nobody set out to build a financial system where the reasoning behind a consumer decision disappears the moment it is made. It happened one sensible decision at a time, over thirty years. What changed is not the obligation, which is decades old, but the standard of proof, and in one market it changed on a specific day, in writing, with a penalty attached.

The origin

How it happened, and why nobody fixed it.

How the record went missing
ONE FILE, BROKEN INTO SYSTEMS NOBODY JOINED
EraWhat changed
Before 1990sOne paper file held the notes, the application and the signed documents together. Poor and slow, but whole.
1990s-2000sEach step digitised on its own. The folder broke into systems never designed to be read together.
2010sSpeed became the product. Explanation moved into conversation, and nothing captured it.
2020sReasoning now lives across email, phone calls and one person's memory, and leaves when they leave.
TodayRegulators ask firms to demonstrate the assessment. It is assembled after the question, by the party being asked.
The record was never deleted. It was never assembled in the first place. This is an architectural gap, and architectural gaps do not close on their own.
Why nobody fixed it
FOUR REASONS, NONE OF THEM NEGLIGENCE
Nobody's job was enoughspanning three systems improves no one's number
Assertion was invisibledescribing the process satisfied supervision
The cost was absorbeda few days here, a complaint there, no budget line
The duties are scatteredfive instruments, met separately, no single artefact
The first three are pressure. The fourth is why a response is now buildable: connecting to the underlying systems is now ordinary infrastructure, not a bespoke build priced per customer.
The pain

Four parties, one missing artefact.

The consumer
THE PERSON WITH THE MOST AT STAKE LEAVES WITH NOTHING
What they experienceWhat is happening
Deciding before understandingThe explanation is verbal, under time pressure, and nothing captures it.
Sending the same documents four timesNo party can rely on another's copy, so the consumer becomes the integration layer.
Never learning why this productThe comparison happened in someone's head; the file records only the outcome.
A dispute that is their word against the firm'sBoth sides reconstruct from memory. The better-documented party prevails, and it is not the consumer.
OBSI consumer inquiries rose nearly 60 per cent in 2025; investigations rose 90 per cent, past 6,100. Complaint volume is not proof of misconduct: it measures how often a matter cannot be closed from the file.

The regulator
A FILE ASSEMBLED AFTER THE QUESTION, BY THE PARTY BEING ASKED
BodyWhat supervision found

Nothing in these findings says the advice was wrong. Each says the file could not show whether it was. That is a different finding, and it is the one a record is built to answer.
The threat

What it costs when the record is not there.

What it costs when the record is not there
THREE EVENTS, THREE SCALES
The eventThe scale
A US payments intermediary fails (Synapse)100,000+ customers locked out of $265M+; no dated version existed to reconstruct ownership.
UK motor-finance redress£9.1bn across 12.1M agreements; liability turned on evidencing disclosure years later.
An Ontario complaint or examinationDays of reconstruction; penalties across 100 FSRA actions, 25 licences revoked or refused.
The pattern is identical: nothing was wrong with the transaction on the day. What was missing was the ability to show, afterwards, what had been true at the time. A gap cannot be filled later, and every closed file is still open.
What changed, and the day it changed
FOUR MARKETS MOVED FROM DESCRIBING A PROCESS TO PRODUCING EVIDENCE
DateThe move
8 Sep 2025Safeguarding, annual-report and review duties in force (RPAA).
31 Mar 2026First annual safeguarding report falls due.
1 Oct 2026RECO annual financial filing begins.
2027RECO monthly trust reconciliation planned.
2028 onIndependent reviews fall due, firm by firm.
Who owes the duty: only a provider holding end-user funds (RPAA s.2(b)). The reviewer may be internal or external if independent of the framework (Bank of Canada Guideline s.5.3). A violation can cost up to $1M, and up to $10M for a very serious one (RPAR ss.48-49).
The opportunity, and why now

The same record answers four questions at once.

What becomes possible, and for whom
ONE TRANSACTION RECORD, FOUR QUESTIONS ANSWERED AT ONCE
PartyWhat it replaces
The consumerA verbal explanation that survives nowhere, and a signature that proves nothing about understanding.
The professionalPersonal exposure on a file whose evidence lives in an inbox they do not control.
The firmDays of reconstruction per examination, and an indemnity renewal argued from assertion.
The regulatorA file assembled after the question, by the party being asked.
The consumer is never charged. The regulated business pays, which keeps the consumer layer free and independent of the party being examined. It is one company, not four products, because the engine does not change between markets - only the rule set it is pointed at.
Why now, and not three years either side of now
THE DUTY ATTACHES TO DAYS THAT ARE PASSING
Why not earlierWhy not later
Before Sep 2025 there was no daily safeguarding record to produce, and no penalty behind it.The record cannot be created backwards. Every month without one can never be evidenced.
Supervision accepted a described process, so a firm would have bought something nobody asked for.Deadlines arrive firm by firm from 2028; the firms that start last are the ones with gaps.
Integration was a bespoke project priced per customer.Once a category has a recognised leader, a firm's default is to buy the one its peers reference.
Nobody can buy last year's records. That single sentence is the whole timing argument, and it is the only one here that does not depend on a forecast.
