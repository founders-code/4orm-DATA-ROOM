How the Investment Works

Most of the money in this round comes in as an agreement to invest now and set the share price later, at the next priced round. Two of the early cheques instead buy shares at a set price today. In plain terms, an early investor puts money in now, and when a later round sets a real price, that money turns into shares on terms that favour the early investor. Here is how each piece works, written for a first time investor.

Buying shares at a set price

The first two cheques buy shares at a price fixed today: 25 cents a share for the first $50,000, and 50 cents a share for the first $500,000. The investor owns those shares from the day the cheque clears. This is the simplest form and there is nothing to convert later.

Invest now, price it later

The three later cheques of $500,000 each are money in now, with shares handed over later. Nobody sets a share price today. Instead, when a bigger, priced round happens, that round sets the price, and the early money turns into shares at that point. This lets the company take money in before it is ready to fix a price.

The discount and the ceiling

Two things protect the early investor when the price is finally set. A fifteen per cent discount means the early money buys in cheaper than the new round. A ceiling is the highest company value at which the early money is allowed to convert, so if the company is worth more than the ceiling by then, the early investor still converts as if it were worth only the ceiling. A lower ceiling favours the early investor. The three ceilings here are $15,000,000, $18,000,000 and $20,000,000.

The best terms promise

The early money also carries a promise that if any other early investor is later given better terms, this money gets those better terms too. It stops an early backer from being left behind by a sweeter deal offered to someone who came after.

How an investor is paid

A return can arrive without the company ever being sold. From 2029 the company plans to pay out forty per cent of its profit after tax. The investors are paid first and in full until they have had four times their money back in cash, and the founding shares receive nothing until that point is reached. After the cash, the shares are still held.
