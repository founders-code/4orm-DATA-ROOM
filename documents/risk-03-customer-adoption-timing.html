Adoption and timing risk

Will Customers Adopt in Time

This asks whether buyers sign, go live and pay on the schedule the funding plan assumes, and whether anyone is measuring the one number that decides the profit margin. As at August 2026 there are no paying customers, no signed contracts and two trial customers in prospect. Everything follows from two figures: 970 signatures needed across five years, and how long a new customer waits for its first usable record, which cannot be modelled and can only be watched.

What you are looking to know

  • Will enough customers sign, and on what timetable?
  • What is the one number that decides whether this is a software business?
  • Where does half of new business come from, and does that channel exist yet?
  • What happens if buyers decide to wait?
  • How will trouble show up before it reaches the revenue?

970 signatures, none yet

The plan needs 69 new customers in 2027, then 184, 259, 214 and 244, including replacing anyone who leaves. Most of the 2031 base sits in the smallest tier, so the exposure is the count of signatures rather than the price.

The number nobody can model

How fast a new customer reaches its first signed reconciliation decides whether this earns software margins. The plan puts 16 setup people in front of 835 customers by 2031, and the honest control is to measure that time from the very first customer.

Half the business through a channel not built yet

Partner-sourced business has to rise from 20 per cent of new customers in 2027 to 51.6 per cent by 2031. No outside reviewer has yet used the evidence pack in a live job, which is the one condition the core market does not meet.

Signed is not live

The funding cheques release against customers being live in production, not merely signed. The gap between the two is where early plans usually slip, so signed and live are tracked as two separate dated milestones.
