The Build Partner

4orm Finance does not employ its own engineers. The software is built by an outside firm, Speer Technologies, under a written work plan with a named team, a fixed fee and a one month exit at every stage. This document sets out what 4orm Finance says about that arrangement and why using a partner, rather than hiring a team, lets the company reach a working product on the small first round of money.

Who they are

Speer Technologies is a Canadian software firm at 379 Shuter Street in Toronto, with a second Toronto office and one in New York and about twenty five people. It builds software, cloud systems and data systems, and it was named to the Globe and Mail list of Canada's top growing companies in both 2024 and 2025.

A team you can name

Ten seats cover product, engineering, security and design, and nine of them carry a real name in Speer's own papers. A team you can name can be checked. A team described only as spare capacity cannot.

What is signed, and what is not

Today there is only a letter of intent. It becomes a final agreement when the deposit is paid, and the handover of the software's ownership to 4orm Finance is part of that same final agreement. Until the deposit is paid, nothing beyond the letter exists.

4orm keeps the sensitive parts in house

The build partner writes most of the code, but the record-keeping core, the two key dates and the filing packs stay with a senior engineer that the funding round pays for. A developer never makes a compliance decision, which is a control an outside reviewer can test.
