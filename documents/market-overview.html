The Proof That Falls Every Morning

Canada spent 2025 and 2026 tightening two separate proof obligations on firms that hold money for other people, and raised the penalties behind both. In October 2025 one Canadian firm was penalised $176,960,190, and much of what it was penalised for was being unable to show its work. Nobody sells software built for either obligation, so both are met in spreadsheets. This overview covers the market and what 4orm Finance is bringing to it. It does not describe how the software works. The obligation itself, the buyers and the working session are in the orientation brief issued alongside it.

What is happening in the market

Two federal regimes moved at once. Firms that hold money now answer to the Bank of Canada for keeping it safe and to FINTRAC for knowing where it came from. Both were tightened inside eighteen months, and neither was phased in gently.

DateWhat changedWhat it means for a firm
1 April 2025Factors, cheque cashers and financing or leasing businesses are brought inside the anti-money-laundering regime for the first time.The population owing a federal compliance record starts growing before anyone has tooling for it.
8 September 2025The safeguarding obligation comes into force under the Retail Payment Activities Act.It applies to firms already trading, not only to new entrants. There was no transition period.
1 October 2025Private ATM acquirers and title insurers are brought in, and money services business registration is tightened.More firms, more sectors, and the same absence of a product built for any of it.
26 March 2026Royal Assent to the Bill C-12 amendments. Penalty ceilings rise roughly forty fold, and the legal test for a compliance programme is rewritten.The test moved from what a firm intended to what it can show actually worked.
13 April 2026FINTRAC publishes its roadmap for universal enrolment.Every reporting entity has to enrol, not only those already registered. The buyer list widens again.
8 September 2028Every registered payment firm owes an outside review, reading three years back.The review will read records being created now. History nobody kept cannot be made later.
2

Why nobody sells a tool for it

The obligation is eleven months old, which is young enough that no vendor has built for the Canadian rules. The categories that look adjacent each miss it for a different reason, and none of them is a small gap that a release would close.

1Accounting packages

Reconcile well, then overwrite the prior state. Only the current position survives, and the question is always about a past one.

2Practice and property systems

Keep a trust ledger that reports today's balance, not the reconciliation as it stood in a past month under the rule that applied then.

3Compliance consultants

Produce a written procedure describing what a firm intends to do. A document, rather than a system that runs every morning.

4Document storage

Keeps files and cannot vouch for them. It cannot show that a file was never altered after it was filed.

So the work is done by hand. In the firms 4orm Finance has spoken to, the check runs in a spreadsheet on a shared drive. It reconciles correctly on most days and still fails on examination, for reasons that have nothing to do with the effort put into it.

3

What it costs a firm to get this wrong

The obligation is not a filing that can be caught up later. A firm either has the record for a past date or it does not, and the consequence runs in one direction from there. Two regulators set the price, and they set it separately.

RegimeRegulatorMaximum penalty for one violation
Retail Payment Activities Act, safeguardingBank of CanadaOne million dollars for a serious violation. Ten million for a very serious one.
Proceeds of Crime Act, anti-money-launderingFINTRACFour million for a serious violation, twenty million for a very serious one, and thirty million or three per cent of global revenue for breaching a compliance order.

Those are not theoretical numbers. On 16 October 2025 FINTRAC penalised Xeltox Enterprises Ltd. $176,960,190 across 2,593 violations, the largest in Canadian history. Four of the six violation types were record and documentation failures rather than missed money: policies that were not tailored to the business, risks never assessed or written down, and registration details never brought up to date. FINTRAC issued thirty penalties in 2025 against eight in 2021.

And the penalty is only the first of four costs

What followsWhat it means for the firm
The findingThe examiner records a deficiency. It sits on the firm's file and shapes the scope of every examination after it. Nothing is paid at this stage and the cost is entirely in future attention.
The penaltySet out above. Penalties are published with the firm named, so the reputational cost arrives with the financial one.
The registrationA registration can be refused, suspended or revoked. For a payment firm the registration is the business, so this is the end of it rather than a setback within it.
The personal exposureA law firm or a brokerage that cannot account for money held in trust answers to its regulator directly, and the partners answer personally.

Why most firms have not acted yet. The duty is young, the first outside review is two years out, and almost nothing has gone wrong for most of them so far. The reconciliation balances on most days, so the risk stays invisible until an examiner asks about a date eighteen months back. That is the gap 4orm Finance is built for, and it is why the buying decision tends to arrive suddenly rather than gradually.

What 4orm Finance is

4orm Finance builds software for Canadian firms that hold money belonging to other people and have to prove, long after the fact, that they kept it where they said. It runs the check those firms already owe, records the result, and produces that record later in the form a regulator or reviewer accepts.

1Never holds the money

4orm Finance does not hold, move or control customer money at any point. It stays in the firm's own account at the firm's own bank.

2Not a regulated financial firm

Not a bank, not a payment processor, and not a money services business, a firm that transfers money or exchanges currency for others.

3Replaces nothing

The accounting system, the practice management system and the banking relationship all stay. The software sits beside them.

4One system, several rule books

The Bank of Canada, FINTRAC, the law societies and the provincial real estate councils all want the same thing proved. One product answers them all rather than one product for each.

Why we are coming in now. The obligation is young, so there is no incumbent to displace and no renewal cycle to wait for. Since March the law asks a firm to show that its arrangements worked rather than that it meant them to, which turns a policy question into a software one. And the first outside reviews are already dated and read three years back, so a firm that starts keeping records late cannot recreate the history the review will ask for. Every month that passes is a month of history a late entrant cannot sell.

Every term used above, in plain words

Safeguarding. Customer money a business must keep separate and prove is all there.

Reconciliation. Comparing what a firm says it holds against what the bank reports.

FINTRAC. The federal agency that supervises anti-money-laundering duties.

Reporting entity. Any business the anti-money-laundering statute applies to.

Registration. The permission a payment firm needs to trade. It can be taken away.
