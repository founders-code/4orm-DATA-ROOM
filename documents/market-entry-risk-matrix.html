How To Read This

HOW TO READ THIS WORKBOOK
What this is, how we built it, and what each tab is for. Five minutes and you will be able to use every other tab in here.
Nothing on this tab is a calculation. It is the walk through. All figures Canadian dollars.
WHAT THIS WORKBOOK IS FOR
The question it answersThere are many groups of Canadian businesses that are required by law to prove something about money they hold for other people. We cannot sell to all of them at once. This workbook works out which ones to sell to first, and says why, using the same test for every group so that no group gets an easier ride than another.
What it is notIt is not a forecast and it does not say how much money we will make. The money is in the pro forma, which is a different file. This one is about choosing where to point the company.
Who it is forAnybody who needs to understand or challenge the choice. If you disagree with where a group ranks, the fifteen scores behind its rank are all on the Matrix tab and you can argue with any one of them.
HOW WE BUILT IT, IN FIVE STEPS
1We listed every group we could credibly serve59 groups of Canadian businesses, each of which holds money or records on behalf of somebody else and has to prove it. Each one is named on the Matrix tab with a count of how many businesses are in it and where that count came from.
2We wrote fifteen questions and asked them of every groupThe same fifteen questions, in the same order, for all of them. Things like: how many buyers are there, can we get a list of them, is it the law or just a good idea, is anybody already selling this. The full fifteen are on the Legend tab with what a good answer and a bad answer look like.
3We scored each answer out of fiveFive always means the answer that is good for us and one always means the answer that works against us. The direction never flips, so a high score is always better, on every single column.
4We decided how much each question mattersNot every question counts the same. Whether it is the law and whether anybody already sells this carry the most, at twelve per cent each. How often the duty falls carries ten. Those weightings are on the Legend tab and they add up to one hundred.
5We multiplied, added and rankedEach score is multiplied by what that question is worth, then all fifteen are added together. That gives one number out of five for each group, and the ranking is simply that number in order, highest first.
HOW TO READ A ROW
Start at the rightThe column called Total score out of five is the answer. Everything to the left of it is the reason for that answer.
Then read across the fifteenGreen cells are where the group works in our favour and red cells are where it works against us. A group can rank high with two red columns, and knowing which two is usually more useful than the rank itself.
Then read the last columnIt names who already sells to that group. An empty answer there is the single most valuable thing in this workbook, because it means nobody has to be displaced.
What we do about itEvery group is put into one of four buckets. Build now is the top three. Next in line is four to seven. Keep watching is eight to sixteen. Not first is everything below that, which is a statement about order rather than about quality.

THE FOUR THINGS WORTH KNOWING BEFORE YOU ARGUE WITH IT
1A high score is not a big marketSome of the highest ranking groups are small. A group of three hundred businesses can rank above a group of three thousand, because ranking rewards a duty that is certain, frequent and unserved rather than a population that is large.
2The scores are judgements, and they are all visibleEvery one of the fifteen scores for every group is a judgement somebody made, and every one of them is on the Matrix tab where it can be challenged. None of them is hidden inside a formula.
3The counts are not all equally solidSome group sizes come from a published government register and some are estimates. The Why tab names the source for every one, and says plainly where no official count exists.
