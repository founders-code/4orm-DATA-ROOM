Ten Things We Sell, and Who Else Sells Them

This paper prices 23 competing products against the ten separate things 4orm charges for, so an investor can see exactly where each revenue line has a rival and where it stands alone. Each line is explained in plain words before it is compared, so a first-time reader can follow it without any background in financial regulation. The finding repeats on every line: no vendor anywhere sells software built for the Canadian retail-payments duty, none can prove a record has gone unaltered, and none can rebuild history it never captured.

Four kinds of product

A point tool answers one narrow question and keeps no record. An evidence platform collects audit proof but never sees money. A reconciliation engine matches numbers but carries no duty. An adviser does the work by hand, which is what Canada buys today. Classing each product before pricing it explains why prices differ so much.

The subscription is the anchor line

The daily sealed record of held money against owed money is the largest line, about $52.3M in 2031. Four British products do the same daily calculation; the far larger evidence platforms cannot do it at any price because none connects to a bank account or a customer ledger.

Some lines have no rival at all

Nobody sells rebuilding a ledger that was never kept, nobody sells a second-regulator configuration on a safeguarding record, and nobody sells a fixed-price pack for the annual report that every registered firm owes by 31 March. The alternative is always an adviser who will not quote until they have scoped the job.

What everyone is missing

Across all 23 products the result is the same: none carries the Canadian rule, none can prove a record is unaltered to an outside party, none rebuilds history from before it was connected, and only four reach both sides of the position, all under another country's rules.
