What Each Price Is Worth, and the Evidence Behind It

This paper tests all five of 4orm's prices against what firms of the same size actually pay for comparable software elsewhere. Four of the five prices sit inside a published or observed range and none sits above the ceiling of its range; the fifth, the partner price, has no comparable anywhere, and the paper says so rather than reaching for a proxy. Prices are compared on what a customer locks in at signature, because that is what the plan collects and what a reader can check. Every comparable is a published price or a real signed contract with its source named.

What you are looking to know

  • Whether each price is defensible against what firms already pay elsewhere
  • How much of the plan rests on the price of the smallest, most numerous tier
  • Why two prices were raised during the review and two were held
  • What a firm faces in cost and fines if it buys nothing at all
  • Which price has no comparable behind it and is marked as an assumption
  • What single thing would settle the pricing question for good

Locked price, not list price

A firm keeps the price it signs at for as long as it stays, and the card steps up once, on 1 January 2028. A small firm signing in 2027 locks about $32,837 for life; one waiting until 2028 locks $36,813. Every table compares signed price against signed price.

The small tier carries the plan

The smallest firms are 631 of the customers and the largest single block of revenue, so the price is tested hardest here. It sits just above the middle of the observed range, and even a 20 per cent cut still clears $100M in 2031, which shows the plan is not leaning on it.

Two raised, two held

The mid-market and enterprise prices were raised because the evidence showed them sitting on the floor of their ranges. The top and partner prices were held, because their evidence is the thinnest and raising them buys little while costing the strongest comparables.

The cost of buying nothing

One mid-level compliance officer costs a small firm two to three years of the subscription and does the work by hand. A single maximum fine under the older rule cost more than thirteen years of the small price, and fines rose to about $20M in March 2026.
